# Project Overview

## Objective

Build, model, simulate, flight-test, and document a commercial model rocket as a compact independent aerospace engineering project.

The project was started to maintain technical momentum over the summer and produce evidence of hands-on engineering work that could be discussed in internship, résumé, and technical-interview contexts.

## Vehicle

- Kit: LOC Precision Graduator
- Project name: **INSTINCT**
- Motor mount: 29 mm
- Flight motor: Estes F15-4
- Launch guide: two 1010 rail buttons
- Motor retention: Estes 29 mm screw-on retainer, model 24020
- Recovery: 21 in parachute

## Engineering questions

The project focused on practical questions rather than merely completing the kit:

1. How different is the completed rocket from the manufacturer digital model?
2. How much does finishing/hardware change mass and CG?
3. Is the finished vehicle stable with the intended motor?
4. Is the launch rail long enough to achieve a reasonable rail-exit velocity?
5. How sensitive are apogee and recovery conditions to wind?
6. Does the observed flight behavior and video-derived ascent timing agree with the calibrated model?

## Definition of success

The final project would be considered successful when the rocket was:

- physically complete;
- calibrated using measured dry mass and CG;
- modeled in OpenRocket with actual hardware changes;
- simulated across realistic conditions;
- launched through an organized site;
- successfully recovered; and
- documented in a reproducible GitHub repository.

All of those objectives were met.
