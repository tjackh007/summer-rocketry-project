# 03 — Construction and Finishing

## Late June through mid/late July 2026

This phase covered most of the hands-on work. The exact daily sequence was not recorded continuously, so the entry groups the known build activities into the order they occurred.

### Airframe and motor-mount work

- Sanded and prepared mating surfaces.
- Dry-fit the motor mount and centering rings before adhesive.
- Installed the 29 mm motor-mount assembly.
- Used epoxy/adhesive for structural joints and allowed full cure time between major operations.
- Continued checking alignment before committing to permanent joints.

### Fin installation

The fin can was one of the most important structural portions of the build.

- Prepared fin roots and slots.
- Installed fins with attention to alignment and equal spacing.
- Added fillets and allowed them to cure before significant handling.
- Rechecked the airframe visually for straightness after curing.

### Payload / coupler work

- Assembled the payload section, coupler, bulkhead, and recovery attachment hardware.
- Added a retention screw near the payload/nose interface.
- Integrated the recovery attachment point and shock-cord hardware.

### Surface preparation and finish

Surface finishing took more time than the original kit-only model implied:

- Repeated sanding and surface cleanup.
- Primer application and additional sanding.
- Black finish coat.
- Orange/silver/white detailing and **INSTINCT** graphics.
- Final touch-up and clear/finish work as needed.

The finished appearance was intentionally treated as part of the engineering record because paint, primer, decals, and epoxy all contribute to real mass growth.

### Motor-retention decision

The project moved away from treating motor retention as an abstract model detail. A screw-on **Estes 29 mm motor retainer, model 24020**, was incorporated into the finished build.

Published retainer data used for documentation:

- Mass: 13.3 g
- Length: 2.96 cm
- Outer diameter: 4.42 cm

The retainer mass was later captured by the overall measured stage mass so it would not be double-counted in OpenRocket.

### Key lesson from this phase

The physical build accumulated mass through many individually small additions. This became important later when the finished rocket measured substantially heavier than the manufacturer digital model.
